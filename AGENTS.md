# Navdhya Sacred Ember — Locked Colour Distribution

My recommendation is to use the five brand colours to establish recognition and atmosphere, while reserving Deep Text for legibility. The distribution below is for the overall brand identity and typical digital experience, not a requirement that every individual logo contain all six colours.

## 1. Distribution by application

Different applications need different ratios. The master distribution is a planning baseline, not a rigid formula.

| Application                | Ivory | Aubergine | Flame Orange | Sand | Deep Ember | Deep Text |
| -------------------------- | ----- | --------- | ------------ | ---- | ---------- | --------- |
| Website / digital platform | 55%   | 15%       | 10%          | 10%  | 5%         | 5%        |
| Primary logo artwork       | 25%   | 30%       | 25%          | 12%  | 6%         | 2%        |
| Heritage seal              | 15%   | 25%       | 25%          | 22%  | 10%        | 3%        |
| Premium packaging          | 20%   | 35%       | 20%          | 15%  | 7%         | 3%        |
| Marketing campaigns        | 25%   | 20%       | 30%          | 12%  | 10%        | 3%        |

These are proposed visual-area targets. The logo row describes a multicolour presentation, not a mandatory mix for every logo variant. A one-colour logo should use only one ink.

## 2. How each colour should behave

Navdhya Flame — `#F16A24`

The signature. Use it for the central flame or motif, distinctive letter details, and important brand highlights. Avoid flooding every screen with orange.

Deep Ember — `#B74717`

The supporting orange. Use it sparingly for inner geometry, fine accents, and selected interactive elements. Do not use it as a substitute for a verified high-contrast text colour.

Aubergine Ink — `#2C2033`

The anchor. Use it for the wordmark, principal outlines, central symbol structure, and dark backgrounds. It keeps the bright orange from feeling overly playful.

Warm Ivory — `#FFF7ED`

The main canvas. Use it for negative space, clean backgrounds, and light logo details. This is the colour that gives the identity breathing room.

Soft Sand — `#EFE3D3`

The heritage layer. Use it for outer ornamental rings, subtle surface groupings, and restrained ceremonial details.

Deep Text — `#2A2421`

The utility colour. Use it for body text and supporting typography where needed. Keep it distinct from Aubergine Ink in the design tokens, even though their visual roles overlap.

## 3. My final rules

- No green anywhere in the core identity.
- Orange is the signature, not the background for everything.
- Aubergine provides the strongest structural contrast.
- Ivory and Sand preserve warmth and negative space.
- Deep Text is functional, not another decorative brand colour.
- Keep the primary symbol recognisable in Aubergine alone, orange alone, and monochrome.
- Verify text contrast and print colour reproduction before production.

Final verdict: Lock the six colour tokens and use 45% Ivory, 22% Aubergine, 14% Flame Orange, 10% Sand, 6% Deep Ember, and 3% Deep Text as the overall brand-system starting point. The real test is whether Navdhya remains instantly identifiable when the palette is reduced to just Aubergine and Flame Orange.
